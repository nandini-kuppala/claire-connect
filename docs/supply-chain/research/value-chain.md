# Value-chain stages and who does them in Tiruppur knitwear

*Research note for the CLAIRE Connect material-tracking feature. Compiled 2026-11-20.*

**Confidence tags**
- **[verified]**: stated in a primary or authoritative source I read directly (government cluster profile, trade association figures, academic field study, official GST material).
- **[likely]**: supported by one reputable secondary source, by search-result snippets I could not open in full, or by several weaker sources that agree.
- **[uncertain]**: vendor or marketing claims, generic industry rules of thumb not specific to Tiruppur, or my own inference from the evidence. Check with real users before building on it.

**How to read the numbers.** Unit counts for Tiruppur vary a lot (see §Q2). One reason is that businesses often register several "units" under common ownership ([Cawthorne 1993](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/295271/files/openu015.pdf); [Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). Treat every count as an order of magnitude, not a census.

---

## Key takeaways for the design

1. **The chain is a network (a DAG), not a fixed line.** Printing and embroidery usually happen on **cut panels**, after cutting and before stitching. Yarn dyeing comes *before* knitting. Garment washing or dyeing comes *after* stitching. Collars and cuffs are knitted by a separate unit and meet the body panels at stitching. [verified, see §Q1]
2. **"Dyeing & processing" is several businesses.** Bleaching, dyeing, compacting/calendering and raising are run by separate units with their own associations. Fabric often moves directly from the dyer to the compactor, never passing through the owner. [verified]
3. **Ownership and location separate early and stay separate.** Exporters and domestic "makers" usually own the material while it sits at 3–8 job workers in turn. Some coordinators own no machines at all: export "merchant exporters", and the domestic "cutting firm" that has only a cutting table. [verified]
4. **Job workers serve many principals and sometimes pass work on.** A stitching unit can be holding three principals' cut bundles at once and quietly sending part of one order to another unit or to homeworkers. [verified]
5. **Units change along the chain:** kg (yarn and fabric) → rolls and kg → pieces by size and component (panels) → pieces, dozens and boxes. Losses and waste occur at every step, and the waste itself is sold. [verified for the unit changes; loss %s uncertain]
6. **Reverse flows are normal.** These include shade rejections sent back for re-dyeing, misprinted panels, alteration pieces, retailer returns, stock lots and waste sales. [verified/likely]
7. **Today's records are informal:** paper delivery challans, WhatsApp, Excel and notebooks. GST law requires challans and quarterly ITC-04 reporting for job work, which gives the app a natural record to digitise. [likely/verified]

## (b) Findings by question

### Q1. What are the stages and sub-processes? (including often-missed ones)

- The government cluster profile describes the canonical flow: cotton from north India is spun in Tiruppur/Coimbatore mills, then knitted in the cluster, dyed, finished, printed, and made into garments "with accessories and embellishments". **[verified]** ([MSME-DI Tirupur profile 2015-16](https://dcmsme.gov.in/dips/2016-17/DIP.TIRUPUR.2015.16.pdf))
- **Printing usually happens on cut panels.** Roy's field study says printing "is done after the garments are cut according to specific designs by the exporting firm". It is "in most of the cases" outsourced, though some exporters print in-house. **[verified]** ([Roy 2009, ISID/MPRA](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). Roll printing also exists (rotary; reactive printing "in roll form and panel form") **[verified]** ([Apparel Resources 2016](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sourcing/tirupur-enough-scope-for-all-kinds-of-printing)). The app must therefore allow printing before *or* after cutting.
- **Printing types seen in Tiruppur:** table and screen, automatic 10–12-colour, robot-type screen, semi-rotary and rotary, sublimation (incl. "3D sublimation" on cotton), heat transfer, digital/DTG (e.g., Kornit), hybrid digital+screen, reactive panel and roll printing. One job-work printer reported 5,000 pcs/day sublimation, 10,000 automatic and 5,000 table printing, serving ~30 exporters regularly. **[verified]** ([Apparel Resources 2016](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sourcing/tirupur-enough-scope-for-all-kinds-of-printing)). Flock, foil and glitter specialty prints, plus the table → semi-rotary → rotary evolution, are **[likely]** ([textilelearner overview](https://textilelearner.net/?p=983)).
- **Processing is a bundle of sub-processes:** mercerisation, dyeing and compacting. Dyeing can be **yarn dyeing or fabric dyeing**, with fewer yarn dyers. Compacting "includes drying or dehydrating, raising, stone washing, and calendaring" to control shrinkage. **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf))
- Yarn dyeing is mostly cheese dyeing in HT/HP vessels. Finishing is still largely **tubular**, with open-width emerging. Around 200 imported tubular compactors and ~500 computerised flat machines for collars and cuffs are installed. **[likely]** ([textilelearner overview](https://textilelearner.net/?p=983), undated)
- **Garment washes and garment dyeing** are offered by Tiruppur process houses: perfume wash, pigment-dye silicone wash, garment dyeing, lava dyeing, acid wash. **[verified]** ([Apparel Resources 2016](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sourcing/tirupur-enough-scope-for-all-kinds-of-printing)). I found no Tiruppur-specific source on enzyme/bio-wash; it is a standard knit finishing step (bio-polishing), so **[likely]** it is offered by process houses.
- **Heat-setting** of lycra/polyester blends: no Tiruppur source found. It is technically required for elastane blends and polyester share is rising (about 40% of domestic garments are now synthetic, [Apparel Resources Oct 2025](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/manufacturing/tirupur-hosiery-industry-sees-20-rise-festival-season-sales/)). **[likely]**
- **Embroidery** is done "by separate embroidery units", which "also stitch labels, if ordered". **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf))
- **Post-stitching operations are separate wage categories:** the 2016 wage pact lists cutting, tailoring, ironing, packing, fabrication, checking, label putting, hand folding and **damage spotting**. **[likely]** (search snippet of [Apparel Resources on the 2016 wage deal](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sourcing/tirupur-employers-association-signs-4-year-wage-deal-with-trade-unions/))
- **Terminology:** in Tiruppur, "fabrication" and "fabricators" mostly mean **knitting** (yarn to fabric). Roy writes "fabricators value their jobs according to conversion rates ... for a basic knitting job", and Cawthorne's diagram labels knitting "Fabricate cloth". In Delhi-NCR, "fabricator" means a stitching subcontractor. **[verified]** UI vocabulary must be localised by cluster.
- **Specialised ancillary trades with their own associations** include knitting, dyeing, screen printing, compacting, narrow tape, cloth stitching, yarn merchants, cotton merchants and powerloom cloth dealers ([UNIDO 2005](https://downloads.unido.org/ot/47/88/4788340/20001-_23161.PDF)), plus collar stitching units and kaza-button owners ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). There are about 22 associations in all. **[verified]**
- **Waste and stock-lot trades:** Tiruppur traders sell "banian waste" and "hosiery cutting waste" by kg (asking prices about Rs 22–54/kg, e.g., [J.K. Mathi Traders](https://m.indiamart.com/jkmathi-traders)). Tiruppur is one of India's established pre-consumer textile-waste recycling clusters, where collection runs through "decentralised networks of brokers, traders, and informal intermediaries". **[verified]** ([CEEW](https://www.ceew.in/sites/default/files/ceew-unlocking-finance-pre-consumer-textile-waste-india.pdf)). Khaderpet sells export rejects and surplus (see Q5).
- **Transport:** domestic dispatch uses lorry/part-load and parcel operators and big logistics firms (Allcargo Gati describes itself as a long-standing partner to the cluster). **[likely]** Rail parcel trains were offered on condition that traders pool cargo ([Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/trade/special-parcel-trains-for-garment-consignments)). I found no study of how bundles move between job workers inside the town. **[uncertain]**: the brief's assumption of own tempo/van, two-wheelers and the job worker's own pickup is plausible but unsourced.

**Integrated exporters vs specialised units**
- "Most of the garment producers or exporters generally confine to stages such as cutting, stitching and finishing activities and get the prior stages done by specialized firms." Integrated units with their own knitting and processing exist "but such integrated units are few in number". **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf))
- Roy distinguishes three forms of job work: (a) out-contracting of stages to specialists; (b) **"in-contracting"**, where sections of one firm are run semi-autonomously and often registered as separate SSI units; and (c) integrated exporters selling spare knitting/processing capacity as job work to others. **[verified]** Design implication: one owner may hold several GST registrations or units, and a single unit can be both principal and job worker.
- TEA today: "Most units handle only one stage", and an exporter with a few hundred machines takes larger orders by sending dyeing, printing and embroidery across town. **[verified]** ([Upstox/TEA 2026](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/tiruppur-s-rise-how-one-textile-cluster-became-india-s-export-powerhouse/article-201077/))
- The hierarchy has direct exporters at the top, indirect exporters (subcontractors producing for exporters) below them, and "smaller jobworking firms who undertake specific stitching, cutting, trimming or checking operations both for exporters and for subcontractors" at the bottom. **[verified]** ([DFID-ESRC Policy Briefing 3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08adfe5274a27b20007f9/60620_Policy_Briefing_3.pdf))
- Garments move "through a series of production units – often as many as 20 or 30" (De Neve, citing Chari 2004). **[verified as stated]**, though it probably counts every unit an order touches, including parallel ones ([De Neve, Power, Inequality and CSR](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08b6fe5274a31e0000b56/60620_Power_inequality.pdf)). A software vendor puts the number at "8 different vendors" per order **[uncertain]** ([FactoStack blog, Mar 2026](https://website-el2.pages.dev/blog/tiruppur-garment-cluster-software-challenges)).
- "Majority of the units are on job work basis i.e. per piece cost is given", and such units need high working-capital limits. **[verified]** ([BEE cluster profile](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/Tiruppur.pdf))

**Domestic vs export split**
- Exports were Rs 46,000 crore in FY26 (TEA). **[verified]** ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/tiruppur-s-rise-how-one-textile-cluster-became-india-s-export-powerhouse/article-201077/)). Domestic sales are estimated at about **Rs 35,000 crore in 2025-26** (SIHMA vice-president, via Deccan Herald) **[likely]**, or "stagnant at about Rs 30,000 crore" (TIDGMA, 2025) **[likely]**. In FY2020-21 (COVID) domestic sales of Rs 30,000 crore exceeded exports of Rs 25,000 crore for the first time (TEA president) **[verified]** ([Indian Textile Journal](https://indiantextilejournal.com/?p=10610)). In 2015-16 domestic turnover was only Rs 6,040 crore ([MSME-DI](https://dcmsme.gov.in/dips/2016-17/DIP.TIRUPUR.2015.16.pdf)). **The domestic share is large and growing; it is roughly 40–45% of output now.** [likely]
- About **60% of domestic output is innerwear** and about 40% now uses synthetic fabric. **[verified]** ([Apparel Resources, Oct 2025](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/manufacturing/tirupur-hosiery-industry-sees-20-rise-festival-season-sales/))

**How the domestic chain differs from export**
- **Associations:** domestic makers are represented by SIHMA (founded 1956, ~435 active members, [sihma.org](https://sihma.org/)) and TIDGMA ([TIDGMA rate notice, 2021](https://www.newclothmarketonline.com/news-briefs/tirupur-domestic-garments-manufactures-association-hikes-rates-by-25/)). Exporters are represented by TEA. **[verified]**
- **Fabric sourcing:** domestic makers increasingly **buy finished fabric** instead of running the knit-dye chain themselves. Western-state suppliers of printed cotton fabric "have set up godowns and offices in Tirupur", selling at about Rs 20/kg below locally knitted and printed fabric **[verified]** ([Apparel Resources Oct 2025](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/manufacturing/tirupur-hosiery-industry-sees-20-rise-festival-season-sales/)). In 2024, processing and compacting units had not revived because "garment makers are able to source imported fabric at low costs" **[likely]** (Hindu, 3 May 2024, search snippet via [PressReader](https://pressreader.com/india/the-hindu-coimbatore-9WW4/20240503/281582360707057)). So **fabric traders and godowns are real nodes in the domestic chain.**
- **Selling channels:** a 1993 study found petty producers selling "to agents acting on behalf of small wholesalers and retailers", and medium firms selling "in out-of-State Indian markets" **[verified]** ([Cawthorne 1993](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/295271/files/openu015.pdf)). Today the channels include distributors and dealers, retail chains (Zudio, Max, Pantaloons) and e-commerce **[likely]** (Deccan Herald 2026 via search snippets). Domestic goods are sold in Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal and Delhi **[likely]**.
- **Khaderpet (also spelled Khaderpettai, Kaderpeth)**, opposite the railway station, is Tiruppur's largest wholesale market. It "caters largely to domestic trade" and sells export rejects and surplus at wholesale and retail rates **[likely]** (Business Standard 2017/2024, WWD 2018, search snippets only). More than 300 shops closed in the six months after demonetisation (Khadarpet association, 2017), and wholesale sales fell 30% in 2022 **[likely]**.
- **Quality regime:** for exporters, the domestic market is "safer" because "shipping deadlines are less and there are no summary rejections" (TEAMA president) **[likely]** (Deccan Herald snippet). Export work carries buyer quality control and social audits, which smaller subcontractors "can rarely afford", so they export "only indirectly ... through larger exporters and buying agencies" **[verified]** ([De Neve](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08b6fe5274a31e0000b56/60620_Power_inequality.pdf)).
- **Design ownership:** "Exporters' orders depend on buyers, while domestic players market their own designs." **[verified]** ([Apparel Resources 2016](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sourcing/tirupur-enough-scope-for-all-kinds-of-printing))

### Q3. How common is it for one business to play several roles? Are there "makers" with no machines?

- **Very common.** Cawthorne documented three standard configurations in 1993 **[verified]** ([Cawthorne 1993](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/295271/files/openu015.pdf)):
  - **"Cutting firm"**: buys yarn, gets knitting, bleaching/dyeing and calendering done as job work, cuts in-house, gives stitching ("making up") as job work, and sells (typically white banians and briefs) through an agent. It "requires working capital only ... and access to (rented) premises with a cutting table". **This is exactly a coordinator who owns almost no machines.**
  - **"Making-up firm"**: same, but owns one "power table" with four seats (overlock, chain-lock, label stitching, rib cutting), a press and a cutting table; sends out knitting, dyeing, calendering and screen printing.
  - **"Combined firm"**: owns knitting machines and making-up, and sends out dyeing, calendering and printing.
  - Cawthorne's design-relevant observation: owners control **yarn purchase** (quality) and **cutting** (fabric wastage), and job-work the rest.
- An early exporter told Cawthorne: "When necessary, I will get the stitching done by other firms but **we will always check, iron and pack the garments to control the quality here.** I have made fairly permanent arrangements with 12 other firms ... none of them are solely dependent on me." **[verified]** This matches the brief's description of the user's father (buys fabric, cuts, outsources stitching, packs).
- **Export-side coordinators:** the cluster has about **1,200 merchant exporters** alongside 800 manufacturer-exporters (2015-16) **[verified]** ([MSME-DI](https://dcmsme.gov.in/dips/2016-17/DIP.TIRUPUR.2015.16.pdf)). Merchant exporters coordinate production without owning the full chain. The exporter is "the parent firm that organizes the whole production process" **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). A vendor claims "Most Tirupur 'exporters' are actually coordinators" with "40–60% of the work-in-progress ... outside the export house" at any time **[uncertain]** ([FactoStack](https://website-el2.pages.dev/blog/tiruppur-garment-cluster-software-challenges)).
- **Reverse role-mixing:** integrated exporters sell spare knitting and processing capacity as job work for others, so the same business is principal on one order and job worker on another **[verified]** (Roy 2009). Dyeing houses have moved forward into garment making **[likely]**.
- **Career ladder:** "An owner starts with job-work and progresses to the level of a successful exporter" **[verified]** (Roy 2009). Roles change over time, so the app must allow a business to add and drop roles.
- **Domestic example:** a small new innerwear firm said "we are outsourcing stitching process" while building capacity **[likely]** (Apparel Resources snippet).

### Q4. Sub-contracting by job workers; one job worker holding material for many principals

- **Multi-principal is the norm:** "job-workers are not linked to a single parent firm rather they work for a number of exporters" **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). One printing job worker serves about 30 exporters regularly **[verified]** ([Apparel Resources 2016](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sourcing/tirupur-enough-scope-for-all-kinds-of-printing)). Smaller job workers work "both for exporters and for subcontractors" **[verified]** ([DFID-ESRC](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08adfe5274a27b20007f9/60620_Policy_Briefing_3.pdf)). **A dyer or stitching unit therefore needs a "whose material is this" view across principals.**
- **Second-tier sub-contracting exists and is often hidden:**
  - Exporters subcontract to subcontractors, who use smaller job workers (DFID-ESRC above). **[verified]**
  - "Another strategy of evading audits is to outsource some production to potentially non-compliant factories ... 'It can happen without permission too, but they don't disclose that it was done in another unit'." **[verified]** ([Crane et al., Sheffield, c.2019](https://www.sheffield.ac.uk/media/8698/download)). Some sub-contracting will never be entered into the app by the job worker, so the principal's view must tolerate it.
  - **Homeworkers:** firms give part of the work to women who cannot commute, to be done at home, sometimes requiring them to buy a sewing machine ([Fujita, IDE-JETRO 2012](https://www.ide.go.jp/library/Japanese/Publish/Reports/InterimReport/2011/pdf/103_ch7.pdf), in Japanese). Within 30 km of Tiruppur and Pollachi, 330 home-based garment workers were documented, about 85% working for export. In Tiruppur, homeworkers did "trimming or stitching sleeves and buttons", through mostly male labour subcontractors, and **82.8% kept records of their work** **[verified]** ([Kara, Tainted Garments, 2019](https://blumcenter.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Tainted-Garments-1.pdf)). A parliamentary submission cites "more than one lakh home based workers" **[likely]** (search snippet).
  - **Inside contractors:** within firms, "intermediaries controlling both production and labour" ("managers", "contractors", "jobworking contractors") run sections **[verified]** ([Cawthorne 1993](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/295271/files/openu015.pdf)).
- **Job worker to job worker transfers** are legally recognised. Goods can move from one job worker to another under a challan and must be reported in ITC-04 **[verified]** ([GST Council job-work flyer](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Job_Work.pdf); [CBIC Circular 38/2018](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/jobwork_circular_38_of_2018.pdf)). In practice, dyed fabric going straight to the compactor and printed panels going straight to the stitcher are **[likely]** routine. No source states this explicitly, but it follows from the specialisation.

### Q5. Shade/quality rejection, rework, returns, stock lots, leftovers, waste

- **Checking happens at every stage**, done "by the units involved in the respective conversion job", with a final quality check by the exporter **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). Quality rejections are handled at each stage **[uncertain]** (vendor, [FactoStack](https://website-el2.pages.dev/blog/tiruppur-garment-cluster-software-challenges)).
- **Shade lots:** standard knitwear practice is shade-band approval after bulk dyeing, laying up only same-shade rolls, and keeping all panels of one garment from one lot **[likely]** (general industry guidance, e.g., [Online Clothing Study](https://www.onlineclothingstudy.com/2013/10/9-step-guide-to-eliminate-fabric-shade.html); not Tiruppur-specific). **The app needs dye-lot IDs that survive from dyer → compactor → cutting → panels.**
- **Who pays for re-dyeing, and how shade disputes are settled**, is not documented anywhere I could find **[uncertain]**. Field validation is needed: whether re-shading is free, what loss is allowed on re-processing, and whether the material is downgraded to a darker colour.
- **Rework and returns reach the bottom of the chain:** among homeworkers, the most common penalty was "refusal to pay her for the piece" if work was late **[verified]** ([Kara 2019](https://blumcenter.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Tainted-Garments-1.pdf)). "Ever stricter quality control regimes" and "last minute cancellations" are cited as causes of jobworker failure **[verified]** ([DFID-ESRC](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08adfe5274a27b20007f9/60620_Policy_Briefing_3.pdf)).
- **Stock lots / surplus:** generic Indian evidence (not Tiruppur-specific) says surplus comes from extra garments made "to cover quality issues", buyer-rejected shipments (quality or late delivery) and unsold retail inventory. Stock-lot firms check about 10% of a lot at random, lots run 1,000–50,000 pieces, export surplus sells 20–30% below the original price, retail leftovers lose 70–80% of value, and labels are often cut off without a release order **[verified for India generally]** ([Apparel Resources 2023](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/surpassing-surplus-business/)). In Tiruppur, Khaderpet sells "clothes that have been rejected in the export market" **[likely]** (Business Standard 2017 snippet).
- **Retailer returns in the domestic chain:** no source found on return terms between Tiruppur makers and wholesalers/retailers **[uncertain]**. Returns of unsold or defective pieces and deductions from payment are plausible and should be supported, but need field validation.
- **Leftover fabric handling:** no source found on whether leftover fabric at a job worker is returned, kept, or adjusted against charges **[uncertain]**. GST law gives the frame: inputs sent for job work must come back (or be supplied from the job worker's premises) within **1 year** (capital goods 3 years), or they are deemed supplied by the principal. **Waste and scrap may be supplied directly from the job worker's premises** **[verified]** ([GST Council flyer](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Job_Work.pdf); [Circular 38/2018](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/jobwork_circular_38_of_2018.pdf)). So "kept by job worker and sold" is a legally recognised path, and the app must record which happened.
- **Waste sales:** cutting waste and banian waste are sold by kg to waste traders, priced by colour and form (white vs coloured, clips vs tube); see the IndiaMART listings above **[verified as listings]**. Cawthorne notes owners keep cutting in-house so that "wastage of cloth [is] minimised", which shows that wastage is a known value leak **[verified]**.
- **Process loss benchmarks:** I found no Tiruppur-specific published norms. Generic costing examples use ~10% knitting and ~12% dyeing loss **[uncertain]**, which seems high for knitting. Treat as placeholders; the separate `lead-times-losses.md` note should own this.

### Q6. Relationships and payments

- **Trust through caste and kinship:** "the owners of job-working units and those of the exporting firms belong to the same caste and kinship, which helps in building mutual trust" **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). Exporters keep "fairly permanent arrangements" with a stable set of job workers **[verified]** (Cawthorne 1993). The app should model **long-running counterparty relationships**, not one-off tenders.
- **Credit and payment risk:**
  - Smaller firms and jobworkers suffer from "lack of access to cheap credit, default in buyer payments, last minute cancellations", which leads to "high firm mortality among ... jobworkers" **[verified]** (DFID-ESRC).
  - Job-work units "have high CC limits as working capital requirement is high" **[verified]** (BEE).
  - A Tiruppur MSME body (TTPK) "represents industry in some default cases vis-à-vis industry – marketers" **[verified]** ([MSME-DI](https://dcmsme.gov.in/dips/2016-17/DIP.TIRUPUR.2015.16.pdf)), so payment defaults by marketers are common enough to need an institutional response.
  - Knitting units reportedly recover job-work tax from exporters in 45–60 days **[likely]** (snippet).
  - Indian apparel credit typically runs **90–180 days**. CMAI asked for the MSMED/43B(h) 45-day rule to be phased in (90 days by Mar 2025, 60 by Mar 2026, 45 by Mar 2027) **[verified that CMAI proposed it]** ([Apparel Resources Feb 2024](https://in.apparelresources.com/?p=216242)). I did not confirm whether that schedule was adopted.
- **Workers are paid weekly**, mostly on piece rates **[verified]** ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf); [Crane et al.](https://www.sheffield.ac.uk/media/8698/download): "piece work predominates in the cluster"). This means job workers need weekly cash, which pressures principals for frequent part-payments **[uncertain inference]**.
- **Agents:** domestic small makers sell through agents for wholesalers and retailers **[verified]** (Cawthorne). There are also buying agents on the export side **[verified]** (Roy surveyed 4 buying agents) and surplus agents **[verified]** (Apparel Resources 2023). Agent commission of 3–7% **[uncertain]** (vendor page). Yarn merchants and brokers have their own association **[verified]** (UNIDO).
- **Records:**
  - GST requires a **delivery challan** for every job-work movement (rule 45/55), including job worker → job worker, and quarterly **ITC-04** returns **[verified]** ([GST Council flyer](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-version-gst-flyers/Job_Work.pdf)).
  - Day-to-day tracking is "WhatsApp groups, paper challans, and Excel", and errors surface "often a week before shipment" **[uncertain → likely]**. This comes from a vendor, but it is consistent with the challan requirement and with general SME practice ([FactoStack](https://website-el2.pages.dev/blog/tiruppur-garment-cluster-software-challenges)).
  - Even Tiruppur homeworkers mostly keep their own records (82.8%) **[verified]** (Kara 2019).
  - "DC book" as a local term: not found in published sources **[uncertain]**; it should be validated with users.

## (c) Corrections to the brief's hypotheses

Brief hypothesis: *"Yarn → Knitting (often job work) → Dyeing & processing → Printing/embroidery → Cutting → Stitching (often job work, per-piece rate) → Checking/ironing/packing → Dispatch & sale; trims purchased separately."*

1. **Wrong order for most printing and embroidery.** Chest and placement prints and embroidery are done on **cut panels**: Cutting → Printing/Embroidery → Stitching ([Roy 2009](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/23469/1/MPRA_paper_23469.pdf)). Only all-over or roll printing comes before cutting. → Model routes as a configurable **graph of process steps** per style, not a fixed sequence.
2. **Missing yarn dyeing branch.** For stripes and yarn-dyed fabric, dyeing comes **before** knitting (yarn → yarn dyer → knitter, auto-striper).
3. **"Dyeing & processing" is 2–4 different job workers.** Bleaching units, dyeing units, compacting/calendering units (790 per TEA) and raising units are distinct businesses with separate associations. Fabric often moves **dyer → compactor directly**. Each hop needs its own challan, loss and turnaround.
4. **Missing post-stitching wet stage.** Garment washing and garment dyeing (silicone, enzyme, pigment, acid wash) happen **after stitching**, and garment dyeing creates new shade lots at the piece level.
5. **Missing parallel component flows.** Collars and cuffs (flat-knit specialists) and sometimes printed panels arrive at the stitching unit from **other job workers**. Kaja buttonhole and button attaching is a separate job-work step after stitching.
6. **"Knitting often job work" is true, but knitters also sell fabric outright** ("knit fabric for any buyer"). Domestic makers increasingly **buy finished fabric from traders and godowns**, including western-state printed fabric, instead of running yarn → knit → dye.
7. **"Stitching often job work, per-piece" is true, but stitching units sub-contract further:** to other units (sometimes undisclosed) and to homeworkers (trimming, sleeves, buttons).
8. **"Trims purchased separately" is mostly true but incomplete.** Some "trims" are themselves job work (collars/cuffs, kaja buttons, embroidery badges). Trims are often **issued by the principal to the job worker** and must be tracked as owned stock at the job worker, like fabric.
9. **"Checking/ironing/packing" is often the step the owner keeps in-house** even when stitching is outsourced (Cawthorne's exporter quote; the brief's own father). It also contains distinct sub-steps (trimming, damage spotting, mending/alteration, tagging, folding, poly-bagging, cartoning) that produce **A-grade, seconds and alter** outputs.
10. **The chain does not end at "Dispatch & sale".** Reverse and side flows (re-dyeing, misprint replacement, alteration, retailer returns, stock-lot sales, waste sales) are routine and must be first-class movement types.
11. **Missing actors:** merchant exporters and buying agents (export coordinators), domestic sales agents, fabric traders and godowns, yarn merchants, waste traders, stock-lot buyers and Khaderpet wholesalers, transporters and parcel services, homeworker contractors.
12. **Units are not one per stage.** One owner may run several registered units ("in-contracting"), and an integrated exporter can be a job worker for others. → Roles and units must be many-to-many with a business.
13. **Units of measure change mid-chain:** kg (yarn, fabric, waste) → rolls → pieces **by component and size** (front/back/sleeve/rib/collar) → finished pieces → dozens/packs → cartons. Conversions (kg → pieces via consumption per piece) are estimates that need reconciliation.

## (d) Scenarios the material-tracking system must handle

All names are fictional. Quantities are realistic but illustrative. Rates and loss percentages are placeholders, not benchmarks.

**S1. Yarn bought on credit, delivered straight to the knitter, partial returns, and output routed straight to the dyer**
Selvi Garments (domestic maker; cuts in-house, outsources stitching, packs in-house) buys **1,000 kg** of 30s combed cotton yarn from Ganesh Yarn Traders on 30-day credit, in two mill lots: **Lot A 600 kg, Lot B 400 kg**. The trader delivers **directly to Kumar Knits** (job work, 30s single jersey 160 GSM, per-kg rate), so the yarn never enters Selvi's premises. Kumar returns fabric in two deliveries: **589 kg** of grey fabric (Lot A, 31 rolls) on day 6, sent on Selvi's phone instruction **directly to Murugan Dyeing**; and **393 kg** (Lot B, 21 rolls) on day 9, also direct to Murugan. Kumar keeps **18 kg** of yarn waste (1.8%).
*System must:* allow ship-to ≠ owner, keep yarn lots through knitting, take partial returns, take job worker → job worker transfers on the principal's instruction, record who owns the yarn waste and whether it is credited, and keep supplier credit due dates separate from material movement.

**S2. Multi-principal dyer, machine batches, and two dye lots from one order**
Murugan Dyeing is holding **982 kg** of Selvi's fabric, **1,500 kg** of Ravi Exports' fabric, and **300 kg** belonging to a fabric trader. Selvi wants **600 kg navy + 382 kg black**. Murugan dyes navy in two machine batches on different days: **B-117 (400 kg)** and **B-121 (200 kg)**. The navy fabric (now **548 kg**, ~8.7% loss) goes **directly to Arul Compacting**, which returns **541 kg** with a GSM and shrinkage note. The black is delayed 5 days because Ravi Exports' order took the machine.
*System must:* give the dyer a per-principal view of held stock (kg by principal, colour, stage); keep each machine batch as a dye lot that survives compacting and cutting; record loss per hop; show expected vs actual turnaround and the reason for delay.

**S3. Shade rejection and re-dyeing loop**
At Selvi's inspection, batch **B-121 (185 kg after compacting)** is visibly lighter than B-117. Selvi rejects it and sends it back to Murugan (through Arul or direct). Murugan tops the shade (claimed no charge) and returns **179 kg** 12 days later. The result still does not match, so Selvi accepts it as a **separate lot** and cuts it for a different size run. Alternative path: Murugan strips and re-dyes the 185 kg to **black**, so the colour spec changes mid-flow.
*System must:* support a rejection that creates a return-for-rework movement, rework without a new rate (or with a charge), extra loss on rework, a colour/spec change on the same material, the lot staying distinct, and a dispute note. This loop must not double-count stock.

**S4. Cutting into components, and panel printing for front panels only**
Selvi cuts **535 kg** of navy (**356 kg** of lot B-117 plus the **179 kg** reworked B-121, kept in separate lays) into **2,400 round-neck T-shirts** (S 400 / M 800 / L 800 / XL 400) at ~0.2 kg per piece, so ~480 kg goes into pieces. Cutting waste is **42 kg** and leftover end-bits **13 kg**. Only the **2,400 front panels** go to Lakshmi Prints (2-colour chest print, per piece). Backs, sleeves and rib stay at Selvi. Lakshmi returns **1,500** printed fronts on day 3 and **852** on day 5; **30 are misprinted** (returned as damaged) and **18 are still pending**. Selvi re-cuts 30 fronts from the end-bits (using ~6 kg).
*System must:* convert kg to pieces by size and component; track **component sets** (a T-shirt is complete only when fronts, backs and sleeves match by size and lot); handle partial returns, damaged returns and pending balances at the printer; record re-cutting from leftovers; record cutting waste (kg) as a sellable by-product.

**S5. Stitching job worker sub-contracts to another unit and to homeworkers, then delivers in parts**
Selvi now has **2,382** printed fronts (2,352 returned + 30 re-cut). She holds back 12 that lack matching sleeves and sends **2,370 complete sets** (printed fronts + backs + sleeves + neck rib) bundled by size, plus **2,400 woven labels** and **2,400 size labels**, to Siva Stitching (per-piece rate). Siva is also holding **1,800 sets of Ravi Exports' polo order** and **600 sets** from another maker. Without telling Selvi, Siva sends **800 L-size sets** to Gopal's six-machine unit, and gives thread trimming of **1,000 pieces** to five homeworkers through a contractor. Siva returns **900** pieces on day 5, **1,000** on day 8 and **440** on day 11. That is 2,340 good pieces, plus **18 "alter"** pieces kept back for repair and **12 damaged** pieces whose damaged panels are returned for replacement. **42 labels** are left over.
*System must:* represent material held by a job worker's sub-contractor (known or unknown to the principal); show Siva a multi-principal holding view; reconcile sets in vs pieces out plus alter, damage and pending, by size; account for trim balances; and handle the principal's question "how many of mine are still with Siva?" when part is actually at Gopal.

**S6. In-house checking and packing, then partial dispatch to several buyers**
Selvi checks **2,340** pieces: **2,310 A-grade, 30 seconds**. She irons, tags, poly-bags and packs **2,304** pieces into **192 boxes of a dozen**; 6 A-grade pieces stay loose. She dispatches **1,200 pieces (100 boxes)** to a Kerala wholesaler via a parcel service (transport receipt number, freight paid by buyer), and **600 pieces** to a Khaderpet wholesaler on 60-day credit. **504 pieces** stay in her own stock.
*System must:* handle grade splits (A/seconds/alter), unit changes (pieces → dozens → boxes), dispatches split across buyers and transporters, stock remaining at the maker, and links to invoices and credit terms.

**S7. Retailer returns and stock-lot sale**
After 75 days the Kerala wholesaler returns **140 pieces**: **110 XL that didn't sell** and **30 defective** (stitching faults). The value is deducted from the balance payment. Selvi re-checks them: 12 of the defectives can be mended. She sells **18 defectives + 30 seconds + 110 XL + 170 pieces of last season's stock** (328 pieces) to a Khaderpet stock-lot buyer at about 40% of the wholesale price, as one mixed lot.
*System must:* record returns (with reason) against the original dispatch, mend/rework after a return, apply credit-note-style payment adjustments, track inventory grades, and record stock-lot sales of mixed SKUs as one transaction.

**S8. Export principal's leftover fabric and cutting waste held at a cutting job worker**
Ravi Exports sends **1,480 kg** of dyed, compacted fabric to Sri Cutting Works (a cutting job worker, per piece) for **6,000 polo bodies**. After cutting, **62 kg of leftover fabric** and **140 kg of cutting waste** remain at Sri Cutting. Ravi asks for the 62 kg back but lets Sri Cutting **sell the waste** to a waste trader at about Rs 40/kg and **adjust it against cutting charges**. Ten months later, 20 kg of the leftover has still not come back.
*System must:* keep leftover and waste as the principal's property at the job worker; record "returned", "kept and adjusted", and "sold from job worker premises on principal's account"; and flag inputs approaching GST's **1-year** return limit.

**S9. Yarn-dyed stripes and collars from a separate unit converging at stitching**
For a striped polo, Ravi Exports sends **300 kg** of yarn to Vel Yarn Dyeing (cheese dyeing in three colours, 100 kg each; returns **286 kg**). From that dyed yarn, **246 kg** goes to an auto-striper knitter and **40 kg** goes to Priya Flat Knitting for **3,000 pairs of collars and cuffs**. Collars go **directly to Siva Stitching**. Bodies go knitter → dyer (wash/soften only) → compactor → Ravi (cutting) → Siva. After stitching, **3,000 polos** go to a kaja-button unit (buttonholes and 3 buttons each, with **9,000 buttons** issued by Ravi), then back to Ravi for checking and packing.
*System must:* split one input lot into several downstream products; handle colour at the yarn stage; converge components from different job workers at one unit; track trims issued to a job worker other than the stitcher; and support a post-stitching job-work step.

**S10. Coordinator with no machines buying traded fabric**
Banu Fashions owns no machines and works from a small office. Banu buys **300 kg** of printed polyester knit fabric on credit from a Surat trader's Tiruppur godown, delivered **directly to a cutting master** who cuts **1,500 kids' T-shirts** (per-piece job work). Bundles go **directly to Siva Stitching**, then to a packing contractor. Banu sells through a commission agent to retailers in Andhra Pradesh. Banu's goods are never on Banu's own premises.
*System must:* support a business with no premises stock whose entire inventory is "at others"; connect a fabric trader and godown as a supplier node; keep the same job worker (Siva) visible to many principals; and record an agent and commission on the sale.
